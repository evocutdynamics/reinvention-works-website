The day everything changed

From emergency systems to identity reconstruction.

Reinvention Works began with a question Shay could not find answered anywhere else: how do you rebuild a life when the old one is no longer fully available?

Shay had his career mapped out as a 911 emergency communications engineer, building systems that kept people safe during their worst moments. Then his vision started failing.

Anti-MOG, a rare autoimmune disorder attacking his optic nerves, changed the shape of everything. Within months, he went from designing emergency systems to becoming someone who needed them. He lost his sight, his career, his independence, and for a while any sense of who he was supposed to be.

The hardest part was not only the disability. It was the identity collapse: the grief of losing who he was, the exhaustion of explaining limitations, and the isolation of navigating something few people understood. Shay is also a single father.

Disability transition is not about “bouncing back” or “overcoming.” It is about building something authentic from who you actually are, with honesty, tools that work, and a community that gets it.

Traditional therapy told him to accept a new normal. Motivational speakers told him to overcome adversity. What he needed were practical tools that worked with his actual capacity, because some days, survival is the victory. When he could not find that support, he created it: tools for when people are struggling, and a community that does not demand performance.

From challenge to calling

Study the problem. Build what is missing.

After his diagnosis, Shay pursued professional training in psychology, integrative nutrition, certified health coaching, Reiki Master certification, and yoga nidra instruction. He did not do it to become a guru. He did it to understand how people rebuild identity, manage energy, and navigate systems that were not designed for them.

Over the years since, he has supported hundreds of people through individual and group coaching, all facing the same invisible questions: Who am I now? How do I move forward when everything feels broken?

That learning became Reinvention Works and the Reinvention Reset™ System: a disability-informed approach that honors capacity instead of demanding people ignore it.

Published author. Stop the Self-Sabotage: From Broken to Unfinished, available on Amazon.

A company, built on one philosophy

Reinvention Works is growing. Shay's philosophy stays at the center.

What began as one person's rebuild, and then a practice supporting hundreds of individuals, is becoming a company that serves more people and more organizations. It is growing without losing what made it work.

Shay leads the method. As Founder & Lead Facilitator, he sets the philosophy, holds the standard for every program, and personally leads the live experience at its core.

The company scales the structure. Programs, partnerships, training, and resources are built so more people can reach the same practical support, whether they arrive as individuals or through the organizations that serve them.

Nothing gets diluted. Every program is peer-led, disability-informed, and honest about its limits. Emotion opens the door. Structure shows the exit.

What I believe

Change is not a character test.

Disability doesn't end your story—it forces you to write a new one.

Your timeline is valid, even when it does not match anyone else's expectations.

Energy sovereignty—honoring your capacity—is more sustainable than pushing through.

The systems were not designed for us, and that is not your fault.

Rest is resistance in a world that demands constant productivity.

Accommodation is self-advocacy, not weakness.

The disability community has wisdom that the medical model misses.

You deserve support that works—not inspiration that ignores reality.

Who I work with

Adults navigating disability transition.

Whether someone was recently diagnosed, reached a new functional threshold, or is still working through adjustment years later, the work starts from the person, not a prescribed timeline.

Clients and partners include

  • People with vision loss, chronic conditions, brain injuries, and invisible disabilities.
  • Adults rebuilding identity after medical changes or a progressive condition.
  • People working with vocational rehabilitation programs, independent living centers, and healthcare systems.
  • Anyone who feels stuck between who they were and who they are becoming.
  • Adults navigating disability transition without formal support systems.

This work is not a substitute for acute clinical care. If someone is in psychiatric crisis, clinical support comes first. Reinvention Works complements therapy and medical care rather than replacing it.

I work best with people who
  • Want honest, practical tools, not just affirmations.
  • Are ready to use methods that honor capacity instead of pushing through it.
  • Understand that transformation takes time and fluctuates.
  • Will do the work while being gentle with themselves.
I'm probably not right for you if
  • You are in acute psychiatric crisis. Please get clinical support first.
  • You want someone to fix everything for you.
  • You are looking for toxic positivity or inspiration-only content.
  • You are not ready to examine patterns and try new approaches.
How I help

Keep the support practical.

Shay supports adults navigating disability transition through peer-led programs, individual reconstruction sessions, guided group sessions, organizational partnerships, and speaking engagements.

The work focuses on identity reconstruction and capacity management, complementing therapy and medical care rather than replacing it. He collaborates with healthcare teams and refers to clinical services when appropriate. All programs are delivered virtually, accessible from anywhere.
